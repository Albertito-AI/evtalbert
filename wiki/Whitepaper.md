# EVT-Albert: reflective practice without reducing a person to a score

Prepared for Albert Tan Sin Hon with AI assistance. Foundational paper: 2 November 2026. Documentation revision: 15 November 2026. This is a design and evidence synthesis, not independent peer review or a psychological validation study.

## Abstract

EVT-Albert is a small command-line tool for recording sessions and reflecting on emotional state, regulation, obstacles, outcomes and self-rated volitional qualities. Its purpose is voluntary self-understanding: helping a person ask better questions about a particular experience. The current contribution is inspectable software that stores records, computes descriptive summaries and exports them. This paper defines those calculations, distinguishes implementation from psychological interpretation, explains the completed source migration and derives a synthetic example with a missing-data counterexample. It proposes usability and measurement questions rather than claiming that the software measures character, improves performance or supports diagnosis. The person retains interpretive authority over their records.

## Purpose, terms and current state

The founding idea is that a structured record can make reflection more concrete. A user can describe what they attempted, what they experienced, what response they tried and what happened next. A useful tool would help them revise their understanding without transforming an episode into a judgement about personal worth.

A **session** is a stored record supplied by a user, not an independently observed event. An **outcome** is a label in that record. The four **volition ratings** are entries labelled purposefulness, persistence, self-control and decisiveness. The **volition index**, or VI, is the current arithmetic summary of available entries; it is not a validated latent trait. A **readiness ratio** is the implementation's name for a count of one state label, not a clinical assessment of readiness.

## Measurement contract and missingness

The contract below follows [analytics.py](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/evtalbert/blob/cc6ccc53c89f79f40592913cba405369c238940d/src/evtalbert/analytics.py), rather than an idealised interpretation of the field names. Let $K_i$ be the subset of the four recognised rating keys present in session $i$, with supplied values $x_{ik}$. The current per-session calculation is

$$
v_i=
\begin{cases}
\frac{1}{|K_i|}\sum_{k\in K_i}x_{ik},& |K_i|>0,\\
0,& |K_i|=0.
\end{cases}
$$

The rolling index at record $i$ is the mean of up to seven most recent $v_i$ values in stored order. The implementation does not sort by timestamp before this calculation. Consequently, import order can affect the sequence, and a rolling seven-record mean is not necessarily a seven-day mean.

For $n>0$, `readiness_ratio` counts rows whose `pre_state` is exactly `alertness`, divided by all $n$ rows. It does not infer readiness from physiology or outcomes. The average time uses only non-missing `sec_to_target` entries. With no recorded times it returns missing rather than zero. The obstacle summary lowercases and counts text before the first colon; it does not infer the meaning of a narrative. The data model itself is not a comprehensive psychometric or domain validator.

These arithmetic choices make assumptions visible. Equal averaging treats the supplied numeric steps and dimensions as commensurable for this descriptive operation. Neither the names of the dimensions nor the ability to average them establishes a common underlying trait, equal intervals, cross-person comparability or stability over time.

## Worked example and counterexamples

Use the repository's fictional three-session fixture. Every session supplies all four rating keys with the value shown.

| Session | State label | Recorded seconds | Outcome label | Each rating | Per-session VI | Rolling VI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | alertness | 20 | success | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| B | fever | 40 | partial | 4 | 4 | 3.5 |
| C | alertness | missing | success | 5 | 5 | 4 |

The label fraction is $2/3$. The mean observed time is $(20+40)/2=30$ seconds, not 20 seconds from counting the missing value as zero. The final rolling mean is $(3+4+5)/3=4$. “Fever” is a literal input code in this fixture, not a clinical finding. The [demo record](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/evtalbert/blob/cc6ccc53c89f79f40592913cba405369c238940d/docs/demo/README.md) links the recorded JSON, CSV, report and source hashes.

Now give a session no recognised ratings. The present code returns $v_i=0$. That zero is an implementation fallback for absent evidence; interpreting it as measured low volition would be false. A session supplying only one rating of five also yields five, like a session supplying four fives, although their informational coverage differs. These counterexamples follow statically from the source and were not newly executed here. They motivate a future explicit missingness policy, coverage counts and comparison rules before substantive interpretation.

A further inference error would be to credit a regulation strategy because later session scores increased. Changed tasks, selective recording, expectations, practice and circumstances could account for the change. Descriptive trends do not establish that the strategy caused improvement.

## Privacy, agency and boundaries

Records are locally stored as unencrypted JSONL, with local configuration. Local storage alone does not protect an unlocked or shared device. The export option named `anonymize_names` replaces the athlete identifier with a shortened salted hash. Other fields, including free text, remain. Raw logs are unchanged. This is partial identifier masking; dates, narrative details and combinations of attributes can still identify a person.

The MIT licence governs software reuse. It does not authorise publication of someone's emotional or performance history. Public examples should remain synthetic or otherwise appropriately authorised, and a research reproducibility package need not contain identifiable records. The current tool is not a full retention, access-control or deletion-management system.

The intended use is voluntary personal reflection. Employer ranking, compulsory assessment, diagnosis and automated decisions about opportunities fall outside the supported claims. A beneficial interface would let users question a summary, mark it unhelpful, distinguish missing from low values and retain or export their own records. These are design and evaluation goals, not all implemented capabilities.

## Related work and implications

Li, Dey and Forlizzi's [“A Stage-Based Model of Personal Informatics Systems”](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~jhm/Readings/2010-ianli-chi-stage-based-model.pdf), CHI 2010, used surveys and interviews to study collection and reflection. Its account of preparation, collection, integration, reflection and action makes clear that a dashboard is only one part of a user's process. For EVT-Albert, this motivates examining recording burden and whether a summary leads to a useful, revisable question. Their study does not evaluate this tool.

Flake, Pek and Hehman's [“Construct Validation in Social and Personality Research: Current Practice and Recommendations”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1948550617693063), *Social Psychological and Personality Science* 8(4), 370–378 (2017), DOI [10.1177/1948550617693063](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617693063), audited published measurement practice and identified weaknesses in validity evidence. Its relevance is methodological: naming or consistently calculating a score does not establish the construct it represents. It supplies no validation for the four EVT-Albert ratings.

## Research programme and teaching

An initial proposed study should test comprehension and usability with synthetic histories before inviting sensitive real-world disclosure. Compare the summaries with a plain journal presenting the same information. Predefine questions: can users distinguish missing values from low ratings, explain the denominator of each metric, recognise the limits of identifier masking, and challenge an apparently improving trend? Measure errors and burden alongside perceived usefulness. Record misunderstandings and adverse reactions, including pressure to optimise the score.

A later voluntary field study would need a clear purpose, consent and withdrawal process, data minimisation and an appropriate ethics and governance review. Any claim to measure a psychological construct would require a separate programme defining that construct, justifying its items, evaluating reliability and validity, and testing alternative explanations. A helpful reflection tool need not become a trait instrument.

The next engineering priorities are explicit missingness and coverage, documented ordering semantics, stronger input contracts, and user control over retention and exports. Evidence gates are regression cases, understandable explanations and independent first-use checks. Measured benefit would require comparative user evidence; causal performance claims would need a design capable of supporting them.

For teaching, calculate the example by hand, then remove three ratings from one row and explain what changes and what remains hidden. Reorder eight fictional sessions and examine why a seven-record summary may differ. Inspect a masked export for information that could still identify its author. Finally, formulate one useful question the tool supports and one claim it cannot justify. Reflection should expand a person's understanding, not reduce that person to an index.
